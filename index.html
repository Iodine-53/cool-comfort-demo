2. What does it cost, and what moves the number?

A full AC replacement in Florida runs $3,900–$8,000 installed in 2026 (Florida HVAC industry data). Your number sits inside that band, moved by five things:

  • Home size and tonnage. Roughly a ton per 500 sq ft in Tampa heat. The final word is a Manual J load calculation, not the old unit's label.
  • SEER2 tier. The minimum here is 14.3 SEER2. A 16–18 SEER2 unit adds $800–$1,500 and can cut summer bills up to 30%.
  • Ductwork. Leaky attic ducts waste a third of your cooling. We check them before we sell you anything.
  • Electrical. Older panels and disconnects sometimes need a swap to carry the new unit.
  • The refrigerant lineset. 2026 systems run R-454B or R-32, not R-410A. New line, new fittings.

The cost of waiting: a dead-August emergency swap runs 30–40% more than a planned install. Overtime rates, no time to compare. One dead compressor in July costs more than a tune-up plan ever will.

Straight answers

Asked in Tampa, answered straight.

How much does a new AC cost in Tampa?

A full AC replacement in Florida runs $3,900–$8,000 installed in 2026 (Florida HVAC industry data). A 16–18 SEER2 unit adds $800–$1,500. What moves the number: home size and tonnage, the SEER2 tier, ductwork condition, the electrical panel, and the refrigerant lineset.

Should I repair my AC or replace it?

Repair it if the unit is under 10, runs on R-410A or newer refrigerant, and the fix costs under a third of a new system. Replace it if it is over 10, leaking refrigerant, or on its third repair in two years. We will tell you which one you are, straight.

What is SEER2?

The Department of Energy's efficiency test standard since 2023. Tampa's minimum is 14.3 SEER2. A 16–18 SEER2 unit costs more up front but can cut summer cooling bills by up to 30%.

What refrigerant do new systems use in 2026?

New systems use R-454B or R-32. R-410A is being phased down, and R-454B has spiked past $70 a pound. That is why recharging an old system keeps getting more expensive, and why replacements beat endless top-offs.

How long does a replacement take?

A straightforward swap takes 4–8 hours, usually done in one day. Duct repairs, electrical upgrades, or a new pad can extend it to a full day.
